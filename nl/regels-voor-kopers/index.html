Out-of-plan building: frontage on a lawfully existing public road is required (Council of State Plenary 176/2023)

The Council of State (Plenary, decision 176/2023) held that, for out-of-plan plots, the minimum area is not sufficient: the plot must also have frontage on a common-use road that exists lawfully (not a private track, farm road or informal passage), and that this requirement has applied since the 1985 decree. Since early 2023 building services refuse permits for plots - including plots of 4,000 m2 or more - that lack such frontage. As of 2 October 2026 the requirement stands and is written into art. 251 par. 1 of the 2026 Code. The Code provides for municipalities' road networks to be recorded and public municipal roads to be certified by decision of the Decentralised Administration; until then individual road recognition can proceed on a technical study. The presidential decree setting the recognition criteria was still pending.

Greek, EU and EFTA buyers: the border-area prohibition does not bind them

The prohibition on property transactions in border areas binds only natural or legal persons whose nationality or seat is outside the EU and EFTA. Greek, EU and EFTA nationals, and companies seated in Greece, the EU or EFTA, need no permission anywhere in the country. This is the only restriction on WHO may acquire real estate that this corpus has on file; other restrictions (special categories of land, islets, public or forest land) were not researched.

Non-EU buyers outside the areas the border-area law lists

The law defines the border areas by an exhaustive list ('exclusively for the application of this law the border areas are ...'). Outside that list the prohibition does not apply and a non-EU buyer acquires property on the same terms as anyone else. Certiplot applies this only where an area lies with certainty outside every listed prefecture, island, former province and former community: it does NOT apply it in the regional units of Drama, Ioannina, Pella and Serres or on Thirasia, where the extent of the listed areas could not be mapped onto today's units (see 'extent not established'). Derived a contrario from the statutory list; no other restriction on acquisition was researched.

Border areas: who needs the prohibition lifted, by whom, and what happens without it

In a border area, any transaction between living persons that creates a right in rem or a contractual right over real estate (purchase, usufruct, lease, etc.) in favour of a natural or legal person whose nationality or seat is outside the EU and EFTA is prohibited, as is the transfer of shares in a company that owns real estate there. Such a person must first apply for the prohibition to be lifted, stating the intended use of the property. The decision is taken by the committee established in the competent Decentralised Administration (Αποκεντρωμένη Διοίκηση); for certain cases (art. 26 par. 3, privatisation/strategic transactions) by the Minister of National Defence, with applications filed at the Hellenic National Defence General Staff. Greek, EU and EFTA nationals, and companies seated in Greece/EU/EFTA, need no permission - nationality of a company follows its seat, not its shareholders. Inheritance is not caught (only inter vivos acts). A transaction concluded without the lifting is absolutely null and void, and criminal and disciplinary sanctions attach to the parties, the notary and the registrar.

Border areas: regional units only part of which is a border area - extent not established, verify before signing

The law names six former provinces (Nevrokopi, Pogoni, Konitsa, Almopia, Edessa, Sintiki). Former provinces were abolished and their limits do not coincide with today's municipalities; no official table maps them onto today's units. Certiplot applies the border-area regime to the units it could place inside them with certainty. For every other unit of the regional units of Drama, Ioannina, Pella and Serres it could NOT establish that the unit lies outside them, so for a buyer from outside the EU and EFTA the answer there is 'unknown - verify with the competent Decentralised Administration before signing', never 'permitted'.

Border areas (παραμεθόριες περιοχές): the current statutory list

For the purposes of the restrictions on property transactions, the border areas are: the (former) prefectures of Dodecanese, Evros, Thesprotia, Kastoria, Kilkis, Lesvos, Xanthi, Preveza, Rodopi, Samos, Florina and Chios; the islands of Thira (Santorini) and Skyros; the former provinces of Nevrokopi (former Drama prefecture), Pogoni and Konitsa (former Ioannina prefecture), Almopia and Edessa (former Pella prefecture) and Sintiki (former Serres prefecture); and the former communities of Othonoi, Mathraki and Ereikoussa (islets north-west of Corfu). Out-of-plan building on plots under 4,000 m2: derogations abolished in 2020, transitional window extended to 30 June 2027

Law 4759/2020 (art. 40) abolished the old derogations that allowed building on smaller out-of-plan plots (750, 1,200 or 2,000 m2 depending on when the plot was created - reference dates in 1962, 1964, 1977 and 1978 - and on frontage on a main road). A transitional window was kept only for owners who had already started a procedure: an application for pre-approval of a building permit or for a certificate of building terms filed within two years of the law (by 9 December 2022), followed by the permit application. The deadline for these pending cases has been extended several times: to 30 June 2025, then to 30 June 2026 (Law 5261/2025), and - after the rule was codified as art. 273 of the 2026 Code - to 30 June 2027 by art. 133 of Law 5317/2026. Pre-approvals issued under the transitional rule are also valid to 30 June 2027. The extension creates no new right: an owner who did nothing by 9 December 2022 cannot build on a plot under 4,000 m2. The road-frontage requirement still applies.

Out-of-plan building: 4,000 m2 minimum plot AND 25 m frontage on a recognised public road

A plot outside an approved town plan and outside settlement limits can be built on for housing only if it has at least 4,000 m2 AND a frontage of at least 25 m on a common-use (public) road. Plots fronting international, national, provincial or municipal roads need 45 m frontage, 50 m depth and 4,000 m2. Residential building coefficient 0.18, coverage up to 10%, at most 2 floors, height 7.5 m, 15 m setback from boundaries. Since 8 June 2026 these rules sit in art. 251 of the Spatial and Urban Planning Code (Law 5306/2026), which codified Presidential Decree 24/31.5.1985 and Law 4759/2020. Area alone is not enough: see the road-frontage rule.

Greek tax number (ΑΦΜ) is needed before buying; non-residents obtain it online

Every buyer must hold a Greek tax registration number (ΑΦΜ): it is entered in the transfer-tax declaration, the deed and the cadastre. A person living abroad applies electronically through myAADE ('Απόδοση ΑΦΜ & Κλειδαρίθμου'), giving a foreign tax number, foreign address, e-mail and phone. Appointing a tax representative in Greece is optional according to AADE's guidance for non-residents. After purchase the owner must declare the property (form Ε9) and pays ENFIA from the following year; rental income is declared on form Ε1.

Electronic Building Identity (ΗΤΚ): certificate of completeness must be attached to the deed

For any transfer of a building or separate unit the seller must have an engineer create the Electronic Building Identity in the Technical Chamber's system and issue its Certificate of Completeness, which is attached to the deed in place of the older stand-alone engineer's certificate. The system began operating in January 2021 and, after transitional periods in which the engineer's certificate alone was accepted, has been required for transfers since 1 April 2022. The certificate is reported to be valid for two months. The identity records permits, plans, any legalised unauthorised works and the energy certificate. Since 8 June 2026 the provisions are in arts. 341-351 of the Spatial and Urban Planning Code (formerly arts. 52-62 of Law 4495/2017).

Purchase price must be paid exclusively by bank means; cash makes the deed void

For notarial deeds transferring real estate for consideration (and pre-contracts and payment-discharge deeds), the price must be paid exclusively through bank payment means. A deed that records payment in cash, or does not evidence bank payment, is automatically void and may not be registered. A stand-alone fine of 10% of the amount not shown to have been paid by bank means applies, with a minimum of EUR 10,000 and a maximum of EUR 500,000 per violation. In force since 11 December 2023.

A government platform through which the notary assembles the transfer file for cadastre-registered properties, pulling documents directly from the tax authority, social-security fund, Cadastre and Technical Chamber. Property transfer tax (ΦΜΑ): 3% plus a 3% municipal surtax on the tax (3.09% in total)

The buyer pays transfer tax of 3% of the taxable value (in practice the higher of the declared price and the objective/tax value), plus an additional tax of 3% of that tax in favour of the municipalities - an effective 3.09%. The tax is declared digitally (myPROPERTY) and paid before the deed is signed. It applies to every purchase that is not subject to VAT, including a newly built property sold by a builder who has opted for the VAT suspension. A first-home exemption exists under conditions. Since 18 July 2025 the rules sit in the Property Tax Code (Law 5219/2025, arts. 22-52; rates in art. 27), which codified Law 1587/1950.

Capital-gains tax (15%) on property sales by individuals: suspended until 31 December 2026

Article 41 of the Income Tax Code imposes a 15% tax on the gain an individual makes when transferring real estate for consideration. A seller who is an individual therefore pays no capital-gains tax on a sale completed by that date. An extension into 2027 was reported as expected in July 2026 but is not enacted.

VAT 24% on new buildings and its suspension until 31 December 2026

The first sale by a builder/developer of a new building whose permit was issued or revised from 1 January 2006 is in principle subject to VAT at the standard rate (24%) instead of transfer tax. Municipal property duty (ΤΑΠ): 0.25 to 0.35 per mille a year, collected through the electricity bill

Each municipality charges ΤΑΠ on buildings and plots within town plans or settlements at a rate it sets between 0.25 and 0.35 per mille of the property's value, collected with municipal charges through the electricity bill. A certificate of no ΤΑΠ debt from the municipality is required to transfer a property (retrieved automatically where the Digital Property Transfer File is used).

ENFIA (annual property tax): structure for individuals and companies

ENFIA is charged each year on whoever holds rights in Greek real estate on 1 January. Main tax on buildings: a basic rate per m2 that rises with the area's tax-zone price (from EUR 2.00/m2 in zones up to EUR 750/m2 to EUR 16.20/m2 in zones above EUR 5,000/m2), adjusted by age, floor and facade coefficients; plots are taxed by unit value. For individuals with total property over EUR 300,000 an extra charge applies per right on properties worth over EUR 400,000 (scale up to 1%), and the main tax is increased by 5% to 20% where total value exceeds EUR 500,000. Supplementary tax applies only to legal persons: 5.5 per mille of value (1 per mille for self-used business property; 3.5 per mille for non-profit entities). Reductions for individuals: 30% down to 10% depending on total property value up to EUR 400,000; 50% or 100% for low-income households; 20% for a home insured against earthquake, fire and flood (10% if its taxable value exceeds EUR 500,000). Since 18 July 2025 ENFIA is governed by the Property Tax Code (Law 5219/2025, arts. 8-17), which codified Law 4223/2013.

Criminal offences: dealing in, or using, immovable property that belongs to another

Section 303A of the Criminal Code makes it a felony, punishable with seven years' imprisonment, to deal with intent to defraud in immovable property belonging to another. 'Dealing' includes selling, renting, mortgaging, encumbering or making the property available, advertising such a transaction, entering an agreement for it, and accepting the property. Intent is present when the person knows, or ought reasonably to know, that the registered owner has not consented, so a buyer can be liable as well as a seller or agent. Attempt carries five years. Section 281 separately punishes possessing, cultivating or using land registered in another's name without consent with up to five years or a fine of up to EUR 10,000 (raised in February 2025). The MFA states that a European Arrest Warrant may be issued.

Civil judgments of Republic of Cyprus courts about land in the north are enforceable in other EU Member States

In Apostolides v Orams the Court of Justice held that the suspension of EU law in the north does not prevent the Brussels I Regulation from applying to a judgment of a court sitting in the government-controlled area about land in the north, and that other Member States must recognise and enforce it even though it cannot be enforced where the land lies. For a buyer this means the registered owner can obtain an order (for example to demolish, hand back the land and pay damages) and enforce it against the buyer's assets elsewhere in the EU.

The 'Immovable Property Commission' in the north: context, not a route to clean title

A commission set up in the north in 2006 hears claims by displaced Greek Cypriot owners for restitution, exchange or compensation. The European Court of Human Rights treated it in 2010 as a remedy of Turkey that applicants must normally use before going to Strasbourg. That decision concerns owners' claims against Turkey. It does not validate sales made by the authorities in the north and does not make a purchase there safe: unless the registered owner has actually been compensated or has exchanged the property, the title in the Republic's Land Registry is unchanged.
