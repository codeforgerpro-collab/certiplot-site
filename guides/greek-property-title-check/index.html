Title check in Greece: how to verify who owns a property

What a title search in Greece covers, which certificates prove ownership and the absence of mortgages or seizures, and the warning signs a buyer should not ignore.

What the title check proves

A title check answers three questions: does the seller own what they are selling, all of it, and is it free of rights someone else could enforce against you after the sale? A contract transfers what the seller had - no more - so a defect in an old transfer travels with the property to you.

Where the records are

  • Hellenic Cadastre: where an area has been mapped, each property has a KAEK, an extract and a diagram.
  • Land Registry: where the Cadastre is still being completed, the older registers by owner's name.
  • The notary's archive and the tax office, for the contracts and inheritance declarations.

The certificates

  • Cadastre extract and diagram: who is registered as owner, the share, the area and the shape.
  • Certificate of encumbrances: mortgages, pre-notations of mortgage, seizures.
  • Certificate of no claims: no lawsuit has been registered against the property.

Warning signs

  • The Cadastre shows an 'unknown owner', or a different area or shape from the contract.
  • The seller inherited the property but no acceptance of inheritance was registered.
  • Several co-owners, one of whom lives abroad and signs through a power of attorney.
  • A plot outside the town plan whose boundaries are described only by neighbours' names.

Questions buyers ask

How far back does a title search go in Greece?

Far enough to cover the period in which someone else could have acquired the property by possession - in practice your lawyer follows the chain back over at least two decades.

Is the Cadastre registration enough on its own?

It is the starting point: first registrations can contain errors that are corrected later, so the lawyer still reads the contracts behind them.
