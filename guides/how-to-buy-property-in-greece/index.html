How to buy property in Greece: the step-by-step guide for foreign buyers

Buying a house or apartment in Greece as a foreigner, step by step: tax number, bank account, title check, Cadastre, engineer's certificate, energy certificate, price and signing.

Before you make an offer

Most problems with a Greek property are visible on paper long before the contract. A careful buyer asks the questions in this order: the area first, then the building, then the title, and only then the price.

  • Get a Greek tax number (AFM) and open a Greek bank account: no contract can be signed without them.
  • Ask the seller for the property's Cadastre code (KAEK), the title deed and the building permit.
  • Look at the area: flood-risk zones, land burnt in recent wildfires, Natura 2000 and other protected areas, the seismic zone. These shape insurance, building rights and resale.

The title: what a lawyer checks

The buyer's lawyer searches the title at the Cadastre office or, where the Cadastre is not complete, the old Land Registry, following the chain of ownership and looking for mortgages, pre-notations, seizures and claims.

  • The Cadastre extract and the Cadastre diagram.
  • The certificate of encumbrances and the certificate of no claims.
  • The acceptance of inheritance, if the seller inherited the property.

The building: what an engineer checks

A sale of a building needs a licensed engineer's certificate that it has no unauthorised construction, or that what there is has been legalised. The engineer compares the building with its permit and plans.

  • The engineer's certificate and, where it applies, the Electronic Building Identity.
  • The Energy Performance Certificate (PEA): required for every sale.
  • The building permit and its approved plans.

The price: what the official figures tell you

The State publishes a zone price (objective value) for each area. It is a tax base, not a market price, but transfer tax is calculated on the higher of it and the contract price. Compare it with what contracts in the same area declared and with the official price index.

Signing

The final contract is signed before a notary, who checks that the taxes are paid and the certificates are in place; it is then registered with the Cadastre. You can sign in person or through a lawyer with a power of attorney. Do not pay a deposit before your lawyer has seen the title.

Questions buyers ask

Can a foreigner buy property in Greece?

Yes. Citizens of the EU buy on the same terms as Greeks. Buyers from outside the EU can buy in most of the country; in some border areas a permit is needed, which your lawyer can confirm.

Do I need to be in Greece to buy?

No. Many foreign buyers sign through a lawyer holding a power of attorney, given at a Greek consulate or before a notary abroad.

What is the KAEK?

The 12-digit code the Hellenic Cadastre gives every property. With it you can find the plot on the Cadastre's public map and ask for its extract.
