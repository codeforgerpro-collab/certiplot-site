Mortgages, seizures and other encumbrances on Greek property

The encumbrances that can sit on a Greek property, how a buyer finds them, and how they are lifted before or at the contract.

The usual encumbrances

  • Mortgage or pre-notation of mortgage: security for a loan, often the seller's own.
  • Seizure: by a creditor, a bank or the State for unpaid debts.
  • Registered claims: a lawsuit about ownership or boundaries.
  • Easements and usufruct: a right of way, or someone else's right to use the property.

How they are found and lifted

Your lawyer reads the certificate of encumbrances and the Cadastre record. A seller's mortgage is usually paid off from the price at signing, with the bank's consent; a seizure must be lifted before the contract.

Questions buyers ask

Can I buy a property that has a mortgage on it?

Yes, if the mortgage is paid off and lifted at signing; your lawyer arranges it with the seller's bank.
